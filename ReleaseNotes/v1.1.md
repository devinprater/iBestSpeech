# iBestSpeech 1.1

Three pronunciation fixes, all measured against the built engine first.

**Two-letter acronym plurals are spelled, not glommed.** The engine holds
"pcs" as an ordinary word in its lexicon, so a two-letter caps run followed
by "s" was looked up as that word instead of spelled: "PCs" came out as
"pieces". Measured over all 676 two-letter runs, exactly 16 glom this way
(PCs, ITs, KTs, PGs, OZs and eleven more). Three-letter runs were never
affected — no such word exists to look up. Each of the 16 now has its own
respelling, because the fix is per-term and cannot be a general rule.

**Five of the sixteen read as one word.** A plural acronym is said as one
word, so where a joined spelling reproduces the letter names' phone
sequence exactly, the entry uses it: "PCs" is "peasseas", "ITs" "eyetees",
"KTs" "kaytees", "OZs" "ohzees", "PGs" "peagees". The other eleven keep
their letters spaced, which reads correctly; a wrong join is worse than a
correct two-word reading. A hyphen is not a join — measured byte-identical
to the two-word form.

**A word after "a" no longer gains a vowel.** "A large zebra" was
byte-identical to "a largee zebra" on 1995, 1998ENG and 2006ENG, while
"large zebra" and "the large zebra" were both correct. An 88-word sweep
found exactly two words that do this; "large" and "Large" are respelled
"larj" and "Larj", which read byte-identical to the original alone, so the
word's own sound does not change — only the article form stops adding
the vowel.

Verified by the SSML suite additions in each commit and the engine
word-path probes behind each entry.
